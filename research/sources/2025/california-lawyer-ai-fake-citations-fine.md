A California attorney, Amir Mostafavi, was fined $10,000 by the state’s 2nd District Court of Appeal for submitting an appeal containing 21 fake legal citations generated by ChatGPT. Of the 23 citations included in his brief, only two were legitimate. The court emphasized that attorneys must personally verify and read all legal citations, sending a warning to the legal community about the misuse of AI tools to generate content without validation.

Mostafavi claims he used ChatGPT to improve his document but was unaware that the system had inserted fake case citations. Legal experts warn that such malpractice is rising, as AI tools often hallucinate false facts, especially when legal arguments are complex or challenging. The case has spurred discussions about the need for stricter regulations on AI use in legal practice.
