Google’s Gemini assistant now learns from user chats for model training by default, requiring users to opt out manually, as reported by independent articles (2025/google-gemini-now-learns-from-your-chats-unless-you-turn-it-off; 2026/gemini-google-ai-training-opt-out). Anthropic’s Claude uses consumer‑plan chats for training by default unless users opt out, per a blog post (2026/does-claude-train-on-your-data), while Business Insider notes that users must explicitly opt in or out, indicating uncertainty about the default (2025/anthropic-uses-chats-train-claude-opt-out-data-privacy-2025-8). Meta’s AI systems collect public and chatbot data from Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp for training, with no automatic opt‑out, as described in independent coverage (2025/meta-is-training-ai-on-your-private-messages-and-instagram-has-been-watching-you-all-along-d6d9d5467c7c; 2026/how-to-opt-out-of-meta-ai). OpenAI’s enterprise documentation states that it does **not** train its models on user data by default (2026/enterprise-privacy), contradicting the claim for OpenAI. No independent audits or regulator reports were found that address all four companies together, leaving that sub‑question unanswered.
