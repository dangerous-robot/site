Independent analyses offer a range of energy estimates for a single generative‑AI query. IEEE Spectrum reports about **0.34 Wh (0.00034 kWh) per query** *The Hidden Behemoth Behind Every AI Answer*, while other work cites **0.0029 kWh per query** – roughly ten‑fold the **0.0003 kWh per Google search** documented by *Search Engines vs AI* and *Energy Consumption of Google Search and Fun Facts*. The National likewise notes “up to 10 times more power” for ChatGPT versus a Google search. However, peer‑reviewed studies such as *Energy use of AI inference, efficiency pathways, and test-time scaling* find **frontier‑scale inference at 0.31 Wh per query**, close to the search baseline, and only on longer reasoning queries does consumption rise to **~13 ×** the search value. Microsoft Research’s *Energy Use of AI Inference* similarly reports a median **0.34 Wh** per query, with potential scaling up to **4.32 Wh** (≈13 ×). These divergent figures mean the claim of “roughly ten times” is supported by some sources but contradicted by others that show a much smaller gap for typical queries. Consequently, a reader would be misled if the claim were presented as universally true.
