- **Training:** a systematic review of electricity demand for large language models notes that "training of GPT‑4 requires over 40 times more electricity than GPT‑3" (*A systematic review of electricity demand for large language models*).
- **Inference:** independent analyses put per‑query use between 0.24 Wh (Google Gemini) and 2.9 Wh (ChatGPT), and annual inference consumption at 15 TWh in 2025 (*The Hidden Behemoth Behind Every AI Answer*; *How much energy does Google’s AI use? We did the math*; *Energy use of AI inference, efficiency pathways, and test-time scaling*).
- **The comparison:** inference now accounts for most of AI’s energy use: "nearly 60 % of total AI energy" (*The Hidden Cost of AI Conversations*) and "over 80 % of AI compute is now used for inference" (*AI Energy Consumption Statistics*).
- **What drives inference energy:** model size, hardware and query length; reasoning‑heavy queries can use up to 13 times more energy per query (*Energy use of AI inference, efficiency pathways, and test-time scaling*; *Energy Use of AI Inference: Efficiency Pathways and Test-Time Compute*).

**Assessment:** Independent sources consistently indicate that, in current large‑scale deployments, the electricity used for serving generative‑AI queries exceeds that used for training the models. The claim is therefore supported.
