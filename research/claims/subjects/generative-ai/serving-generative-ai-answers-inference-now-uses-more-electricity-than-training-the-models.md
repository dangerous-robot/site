**Sub‑question coverage:** All four sub‑questions are addressed by the source pool.

- **Training electricity figures (sq1):** The systematic review notes the massive electricity demand of training large models, e.g., “training of GPT‑4 requires over 40 times more electricity than GPT‑3” (2026/s1364032125008329).
- **Inference electricity figures (sq2):** Multiple independent analyses report per‑query energy use ranging from 0.24 Wh (Google Gemini) to 2.9 Wh (ChatGPT) and aggregate annual consumption of 15 TWh in 2025 (2026/ai-energy-use; 2025/measuring‑the‑environmental‑impact‑of‑ai‑inference; 2026/s2542435126001145).
- **Direct comparisons (sq3):** Both the IEEE Spectrum article and the Dalhousie blog highlight that inference now accounts for the majority of AI’s energy use—“nearly 60 % of total AI energy” (2025/the‑hidden‑cost‑of‑ai‑conversations) and “over 80 % of AI compute is now used for inference” (2026/ai‑energy‑consumption).
- **Key factors influencing inference energy (sq4):** The papers on inference efficiency detail how model size, hardware, and query length (especially reasoning‑heavy queries) can increase per‑query consumption up to 13‑fold (2026/s2542435126001145; 2025/energy‑use‑of‑ai‑inference‑efficiency‑pathways‑and‑test‑time‑compute).

**Assessment:** Independent sources consistently indicate that, in current large‑scale deployments, the electricity used for serving generative‑AI queries exceeds that used for training the models. The claim is therefore supported.

**Takeaway:** Inference now dominates AI’s electricity consumption, out‑pacing training.
