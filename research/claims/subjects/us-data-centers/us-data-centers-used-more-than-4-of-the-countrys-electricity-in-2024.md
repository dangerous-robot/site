Multiple independent analyses estimate that U.S. data centers consumed between 183 TWh and 192 TWh of electricity in 2024, which corresponds to a share of the nation’s total electricity use ranging from just over 4% to 4.7%. The Pew Research Center report notes "U.S. data centers consumed 183 terawatt-hours of electricity in 2024, representing over 4% of the country’s total electricity consumption"*What We Know About Energy Use at U.S. Data Centers Amid the AI Boom*. The Electric Choice report quantifies consumption at 192 TWh, "4.7% of total U.S. electricity"*Data Center Facilities and Electricity Consumption in the U.S.*. An article in Utility Dive repeats the figure of 183 TWh and "over 4%"*US Data Centers Could Quadruple Water Use by 2028, Double Electricity Use by 2030*. Earlier independent modeling by Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory and an arXiv study also place 2023‑2024 data‑center electricity use at roughly 4.4‑4.6% of total U.S. consumption*2024 United States Data Center Energy Usage Report*; *Environmental Burden of United States Data Centers in the Artificial Intelligence Era*. Together, these sources consistently support the claim that data centers used more than 4% of U.S. electricity in 2024. No source in the pool discusses any retraction, correction, or dispute of these estimates, leaving that sub‑question unanswered.
