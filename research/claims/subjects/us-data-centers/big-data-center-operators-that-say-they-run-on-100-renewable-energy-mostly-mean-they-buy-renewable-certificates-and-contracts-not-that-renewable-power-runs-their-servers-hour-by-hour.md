First‑party disclosures from operators such as Iron Mountain describe their power as “100% matched renewable energy,” which conventionally means the use of renewable‑energy certificates (RECs) rather than on‑site, hour‑by‑hour renewable generation. Independent analyses echo this interpretation: the Uptime Institute’s *Renewable Energy for Data Centers* report warns that reliance on RECs is a common source of green‑washing risk, and the Cedara article *Tracking the Transition to Renewable Energy Across Data Centers* notes that many large providers achieve 100% renewable targets through contracts and certificates, not direct supply. CoreSite’s *Colocation and Renewable Energy Certificates* piece explicitly explains that RECs let customers claim renewable power without direct access to renewable sources. The *Top 10 Sustainable Data Centre Providers* list cites Google’s “matched” renewable claims, reinforcing the prevalence of certificate‑based accounting. The EPA’s Green Power Partnership framework, described in the Wikipedia article, further defines “green power” as electricity purchased from renewable generators, again indicating a procurement model rather than on‑site generation. While a few operators (e.g., Switch) report direct renewable use, the bulk of U.S. data centre operators rely on RECs or power purchase agreements. No source in the pool discusses any retraction, correction, or clarification of 100% renewable claims by these operators.
