**Sub‑question coverage**:

- sq1 – First‑party disclosures (Iron Mountain, Meta) state that they “match 100% renewable energy,” but they do not specify whether matching is done on an annual basis or hour‑by‑hour. The Digital Realty report similarly reports renewable usage percentages without methodological detail.
- sq2 – No independent ESG rating source directly assesses whether US data‑center renewable claims are based on real‑time or annual matching. The Google sustainability report claims a goal of carbon‑free energy every hour, but this is a stated ambition rather than an independent verification of current practice.
- sq3 – Sources do not enumerate the specific renewable certificates (RECs, I‑RECs) used by US data centers nor describe their issuance mechanisms. The regulation‑focused blog notes a US emphasis on cost allocation rather than certificate granularity.
- sq4 – An independent letter from a coalition of state attorneys general alleges that major tech firms mislead by using unbundled RECs to claim 100% renewable energy, implying reliance on annual certificate matching rather than real‑time renewable consumption. This indicates disputes over the interpretation of such claims.

Overall, the evidence shows that many US data‑center operators publicly claim 100% renewable energy through certificate matching, which is typically understood as an annual accounting practice. At the same time, some operators (e.g., Google) express goals of hour‑by‑hour carbon‑free power, and there is documented contention from regulators about the adequacy of certificate‑based claims. Because the sources present both supporting and contradictory perspectives, the claim is **mixed**.

**Takeaway**: US data‑center “100% renewable” statements often rely on annual certificate matching, but the practice is contested and not uniformly verified as hour‑by‑hour renewable power.
